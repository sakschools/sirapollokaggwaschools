Nyonyintono Audrey, a Primary Six pupil at Sir Apollo Kaggwa Primary School Kira, has delivered a memorable performance for Uganda at the XI Africa Aquatics Zone III Championships in Kampala, winning two bronze medals and setting three personal bests. Her achievement is a proud moment for Sir Apollo Kaggwa Schools and a reflection of the promise she continues to show as a young swimmer.

Competing in the girls’ 12-and-under category, Audrey faced strong opposition from across the region. She earned podium finishes in the 100-metre butterfly and 200-metre freestyle before placing fourth in the 200-metre individual medley, demonstrating her versatility across three demanding events.

![](/img/1366956.jpg)

Her medal-winning campaign began on day two in the 100-metre short-course butterfly. Audrey clocked 1:14.53 to claim bronze for Uganda, improving her previous personal best of 1:17.48 by 2.95 seconds.

Kenya’s Kimani Katja won gold in 1:11.92, while Uganda’s Nabatanzi Mushira secured silver in 1:12.88. Mushira, a student at Seroma High School and Uganda’s national champion in the girls’ under-12 100-metre butterfly, added another medal to the host nation’s tally.

Katja, Kenya’s national champion in the same event, continued her strong championships campaign after previously winning silver in the 400-metre individual medley. The competitive final provided Audrey with valuable experience against swimmers capable of producing fast times on the regional stage.

Audrey returned to the podium in the girls’ 12-and-under 200-metre short-course freestyle, clocking 2:27.23 to secure her second bronze medal. She improved her previous best of 2:36.29 by an impressive 9.06 seconds, recording the largest time reduction among her three individual events.

Katja claimed gold in 2:26.41, while compatriot Diamtidis Athena took silver in 2:26.76. Audrey was Uganda’s only swimmer on the podium in this event, finishing just 0.47 seconds behind Athena and 0.82 seconds behind Katja.

The narrow margins highlighted the competitiveness of the race and Audrey’s ability to challenge the leading swimmers. Her second medal also strengthened an encouraging campaign in which every individual appearance brought a new personal best.

On day three, Audrey competed in the girls’ 12-and-under 200-metre short-course individual medley, finishing fourth in 2:49.07. Although she narrowly missed another medal, she improved her previous best of 2:53.00 by 3.93 seconds.

The individual medley tests a swimmer’s ability across butterfly, backstroke, breaststroke and freestyle. Audrey’s improved time therefore provided further evidence of her development across the different strokes and rounded off a productive championships campaign.

Katja won gold in 2:40.42, Mushira took silver in 2:41.32, and Kenya’s Hassan Aisha secured bronze in 2:47.10. Audrey finished 1.97 seconds behind Aisha, narrowly missing the podium despite another personal best.

Across her three races, Audrey collected two bronze medals and recorded three personal bests. For a swimmer making her first appearance at the championships, the results provided a valuable combination of medal-winning success, experience and measurable improvement.

> “I am proud to represent my country this year. Being the first time makes it extra special. This is just the beginning of what's to come,” - **Audrey said**.

![](/img/1365504.jpg)

Her performance formed part of a successful campaign for Uganda, which secured its fourth consecutive Africa Aquatics Zone III Championships title and sixth overall across the competition’s 11 editions. The achievement further cemented Uganda’s position as the region’s most successful swimming nation.

Uganda won the inaugural championship in 2015 and claimed the title again in 2021 when it hosted the competition at Kampala International School Uganda. Its latest triumph extended an impressive run of success, although the country is yet to win the championship in either Rwanda or Tanzania. With Tanzania announced as the host of the 12th edition, Uganda will look to begin preparations to defend its title and extend its winning streak on foreign soil.

Audrey’s contribution to Uganda’s latest success adds to an impressive record at school-level competitions. In 2025, she won individual medals at the National Primary Schools Swimming Gala and emerged as the best performer for Sir Apollo Kaggwa Schools.

She also won all her races at the 28 **Sir Apollo Kaggwa Schools and City Parents’ School Annual Swimming Gala**, helping Sir Apollo Kaggwa Primary School Kira finish third overall. Sir Apollo Kaggwa Primary School Nakasero emerged as champions, while Sir Apollo Kaggwa Primary School Mengo finished as runners-up.

Audrey’s progress is particularly encouraging because she moved from the 9–10 age category into the 12-and-under group this year. The transition has brought her into competition with older swimmers, requiring her to adapt to stronger opposition while continuing to refine her technique.

Her results in Kampala suggest she is responding well to that challenge. Improving her times in every event while winning two medals for Uganda is a strong foundation for her continued development, and we are proud to see her represent both our school community and her country.

Audrey is not the only swimmer with connections to Sir Apollo Kaggwa Schools making an impression at the championships. Former learner and swimmer Muganzi Tyra, who completed her primary education at Fairways Primary School Kireka, is also representing Uganda at this year’s competition.

Tyra enjoyed an outstanding campaign at the 2025 Africa Aquatics Zone III Championships in Nairobi, Kenya. She finished as the second-best swimmer in the girls’ 13–14 age category with 19 medal points, demonstrating her strength across several events.

![](/img/1366957.jpg)

Her individual medals included gold in the 50-metre, 100-metre and 200-metre butterfly, alongside bronze in the 800-metre freestyle. She also contributed to Uganda’s relay success, helping the country win gold in the mixed under-14 4x50-metre medley relay and silver in both the girls’ 4x50-metre freestyle and medley relays.

Now competing in the 15–16 age category, Tyra has continued to perform strongly against older swimmers, winning five gold and two silver medals for Uganda this year. Her progress demonstrates the value of continued training and competitive exposure as young athletes advance through the age groups.

![](/img/1366989.jpg)

Another former learner and swimmer , Husnah Kukundakwe, who attended Sir Apollo Kaggwa Primary School Mengo, also attended this year’s championships as a guest. Her own achievements have helped place Uganda’s swimming talent on the international map.

Husnah made history at the 2026 Commonwealth Games in Glasgow by winning silver in the women’s SB8 100-metre breaststroke. The result secured Uganda’s first-ever Commonwealth Games swimming medal, marking a landmark achievement for the country’s aquatic sport.

![](/img/1366996.jpg)
